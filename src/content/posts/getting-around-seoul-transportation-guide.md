Seoul might be one of the easiest big cities in the world to
navigate. Between the <a href="/vocab#jihacheol" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">지하철</a>
(*jihacheol*), subway, buses, taxis, and bikes, you're never more than
a few minutes from your next ride — and once you get the fare logic
down, it's cheap too. Here's the short version.

## Subway: your main workhorse

Seoul's subway is fast, spotless, and covers basically everywhere
you'd want to go. Base <a href="/vocab#yogeum" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">요금</a>
(*yogeum*), fare, is ₩1,550 with a T-money card (a bit more for a
paper ticket). Lines are color-coded and every station has English
signage, so it's genuinely tourist-friendly. Trains run from around
5:30am to just past midnight.

## Bus: fills in the gaps

<a href="/vocab#beoseu" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">버스</a>
(*beoseu*), buses, cost about ₩1,500 with T-money and cover routes
the subway doesn't quite reach. Blue buses run the main trunk routes,
green ones handle shorter local hops. One tip: tap your card both
getting on and getting off — that's how the free <a href="/vocab#hwanseung" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">환승</a>
(*hwanseung*), transfer, discount kicks in when you switch between bus
and subway within 30 minutes.

## Taxi: fast, and cheaper than you'd think

A standard <a href="/vocab#taeksi" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">택시</a>
(*taeksi*), taxi, starts at ₩4,800 for the first 1.6km, metered up
from there. It's a solid option late at night or with luggage. Just
know that fares jump 20–40% between 10pm and 4am, so a midnight ride
costs noticeably more than the same trip at noon.

## City bikes (Ttareungyi): cheap and everywhere

Seoul's public bikes, <a href="/vocab#ttareungi" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">따릉이</a>
(*Ttareungi*), are parked at thousands of docking stations across the
city — handy for short hops or a scenic ride along the Han River.
Rentals are inexpensive and bookable right from the app, no
subscription required if you're just visiting.

<img src="/images/posts/seoul-transportation-bicycle.png" alt="A row of Ttareungyi rental bikes docked along the Han River" class="not-prose my-8 w-full max-w-lg mx-auto rounded-xl object-cover" />

## The easiest way to pay for it all

Get a T-money card (about ₩3,000, sold at any convenience store) and
load it with cash. It works across subway, bus, and most taxis, and
gives you the transfer discount automatically. For longer stays, Seoul
also offers short-term unlimited transit passes if you're planning to
ride a lot in a short window — worth checking the current options
once you land, since these details do shift from year to year.

That's really all you need to move around Seoul like a local from day
one.

If you've scrolled Korean Instagram or Threads this month, you've seen a
lot of people saying one thing: *they want a nutty latte*. The word they
keep using is
<a href="/vocab#kkosupda" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">꼬숩다</a>
(*kkosupda*), and the drink it's attached to, the **꼬숩라떼
(kkosup latte)**, has turned into fall 2026's biggest coffee trend in Korea.

## What "kkosup" means

<a href="/vocab#gosohada" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">고소하다</a>
(*gosohada*) is one of those Korean taste words English doesn't have a clean
match for. It's the toasty, nutty richness of sesame oil, roasted grain tea,
peanuts, or really good whole milk. 꼬숩다 is the softer, slightly playful,
dialect-tinged version of it: think "nutty" said with a smile.

So a kkosup latte isn't a flavor you add. There's no nut syrup. It's a latte
where the **milk itself** tastes rich, dense, and a little savory, with
enough body that it doesn't fall apart as the ice melts.

## Why now: the watery iced latte problem

Korea runs on iced coffee.
<a href="/vocab#a-a" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">아아</a>
(*a-a*, iced americano) is the default order even in January. Iced lattes are
just as popular, and they share the same flaw: ten minutes in, the ice has
melted and you're drinking beige water.

![Two iced lattes side by side in clear plastic cups: on the left, a pale, watery latte with melted ice and condensation; on the right, a thick, creamy latte with dark espresso swirling into dense milk](/images/posts/kkosup-latte-watery-vs-thick.jpg)

<p class="not-prose -mt-4 mb-8 text-xs text-gray-400">Left: a regular iced latte ten minutes in. Right: what kkosup fans are after. AI-generated illustration.</p>

This trend didn't start with a brand campaign. Home baristas started posting
their fixes online:

- **Freeze-concentrating milk.** Freeze a carton of milk, turn it upside
  down over a cup, and let it thaw slowly. The first half that drips out is
  the milk solids, fat, and sugar. Most of the water stays behind as ice. The
  technique got famous when Canadian barista Ben Put used it at the 2017
  World Barista Championship.
- **Cutting the milk with heavy cream.**
- **Adding a spoon of
  <a href="/vocab#yeonyu" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">연유</a>
  (*yeonyu*), condensed milk,** for body and sweetness.

Posts about the "nutty latte you can't stop thinking about" kept multiplying,
and Kang Min-kyung of the duo Davichi gave the trend a push by posting about
it herself.

## The chains caught on fast

By September, Korean coffee chains had put their own versions on the menu:

- **Mega MGC Coffee — House Milk Latte (하우스밀크라떼).** Launched
  September 3 for the fall latte season. It uses Mega's own "house milk"
  blend, is **iced only**, and costs **₩3,500** for a 591ml cup (about
  $2.50). It sold over **one million cups in its first two weeks**.
- **Ten Percent Coffee — Signature Latte,** built around a denser milk base.
- **Oozy Coffee — Deep Cafe Latte,** pitched on its deep, nutty flavor.

Korean reviews of the Mega version are split. Plenty of people love how rich
it is. Others find it mild on the coffee side and add a shot
(<a href="/vocab#syat-chuga" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">샷 추가</a>,
*syat chuga*), which is ₩300 extra. If you like your latte coffee-forward,
order it that way from the start.

## Ordering one in Korea

- **Easiest:** any Mega MGC Coffee. There's one on nearly every block in
  Seoul, and most have a kiosk with an English option. Look for **하우스밀크라떼**.
- **Say it out loud:** "하우스밀크라떼 하나, 샷 추가요" (*hauseu milk latte
  hana, syat chuga-yo*) is "One House Milk Latte, with an extra shot, please."
- **Watch the caffeine** if you're sensitive: the Mega cup lists 118mg, and
  more with the extra shot. A decaf swap is ₩500.
- **Independent cafés** in Seongsu, Yeonnam-dong, and Mangwon are
  putting "고소한 라떼" (nutty latte) and "농축우유 라떼" (concentrated-milk
  latte) on their chalkboards. Those are the ones to try if you want the
  freeze-concentrated version done by hand.

## Make it at home

You don't need to fly to Seoul for this:

1. Freeze a 1L carton of whole milk solid (overnight).
2. Set it upside down on a jar in the fridge and let it thaw slowly.
3. Stop once about half has dripped through. That's your concentrated milk.
   Throw out the pale, icy remainder.
4. Pour it over ice with a double shot of espresso. Stir.

It keeps for a few days in the fridge, and it stays thick even when the ice
melts, which is the whole point.

## Why it matters

Korea goes through a new food craze every few months, from
[dalgona coffee to Dubai chocolate](/blog/korea-viral-food-trends-decade-timeline).
Most start in a café or a brand campaign and spread from there. The kkosup
latte went the other way. Regular people complained about their coffee
online, figured out a fix at home, and within a few weeks the biggest budget
chain in the country was selling a million cups of it. In Korea's crowded
coffee market, that's how quickly customers can now set the menu.

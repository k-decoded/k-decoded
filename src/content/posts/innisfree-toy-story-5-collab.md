innisfree's latest <a href="/vocab#kollabo" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">콜라보</a> (*kollabo*) run pairs the brand with
Disney·Pixar's Toy Story 5, landing across Korea and the rest of Asia
this past May as a <a href="/vocab#hanjeongpan" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">한정판</a> (*hanjeongpan*), or limited edition — timed
ahead of the film's own summer 2026 theatrical release, riding the same
wave of Toy Story attention rather than launching into a quiet market.
That kind of movie-tie-in timing is standard for character collabs: get
the packaging out while the fandom is already paying attention, sell
through the limited run, and move on. Two pieces from the set, tried
firsthand below.

## Daily UV Invisible Barrier Sun Serum SPF50+ PA++++

This is a <a href="/vocab#jaoeseon-chadanje" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">자외선 차단제</a> (*jaoeseon chadanje*) built to feel like a
skincare step rather than a sunscreen — a serum-gel with an "invisible
barrier" finish that's dewy rather than matte. In actual use, it's
genuinely lightweight with a watery texture, and it skips the greasy,
heavy-on-skin feeling that a lot of sunscreens default to. There's
minimal white cast left behind too — worth the purchase on that alone,
since white cast is usually the single biggest complaint about any
sunscreen.

The formula behind that finish leans on Jeju-grown green tea for
antioxidant support, hyaluronic acid capsules for hydration, and
panthenol plus madecassoside for barrier support, with 1-second
<a href="/vocab#heupsu" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">흡수</a> (*heupsu*), or absorption, as the headline claim — sink in fast
instead of sitting on the surface. Active filters are avobenzone (2.5%),
homosalate (5.0%), and octisalate (4.5%), making it a chemical sunscreen
rather than a mineral one.

One thing worth flagging if you go looking for this yourself: innisfree's
US site separately lists a similarly-formulated **Daily UV Invisible
Barrier Sunscreen Serum, SPF 47** as a standing (non-collab) product —
same ingredient story, different SPF number and packaging. The Toy
Story 5 tube is specifically the SPF50+ PA++++ Asia-market version in
the collab artwork; related formulas, not identical listings, so
double-check which one you're actually ordering.

## No-Sebum Mineral Powder

The second piece is innisfree's long-running No-Sebum Mineral Powder — a
talc-free, loose setting powder built to absorb <a href="/vocab#piji" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">피지</a> (*piji*), or sebum,
particularly around the T-zone — here in two Toy Story-themed jars: one
in green with the three-eyed Alien design, the other in orange with what
looks like Slinky Dog.

## Worth knowing if you're chasing this drop

Because it's a <a href="/vocab#hanjeongpan" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">한정판</a> tied to a specific movie-release window, stock is
genuinely uncertain at this point — availability isn't guaranteed the
way it would be for innisfree's core lineup, so don't be surprised if
some colorways or sets are already running low or resale-only by the
time you go looking.

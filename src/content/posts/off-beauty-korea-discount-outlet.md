We noticed it before we knew what it was: another <a href="/vocab#opeu-byuti" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">오프뷰티</a>
(*opeu byuti*), Off Beauty, storefront, in a neighborhood that didn't
have one the last time we were there. Then another. It's the kind of
store that seems to be multiplying every time we're back in Korea, and
once we actually shopped one, it was obvious why — the discounts are
real, the stock is genuinely useful, and every visit feels a little
like a treasure hunt.

## What Off Beauty actually is

Off Beauty is Korea's first discount beauty outlet chain, run by Q&B
International, a subsidiary of Daemyung Chemical. It opened its
flagship store in Seoul's Gwangjang Market in May 2025 and has grown
fast since — over 30 stores by mid-2026 by some counts, 40+ by others,
with a stated target of 100 stores nationwide by the end of 2026,
including an expansion to Jeju. Seoul locations include Gwangjang
Market, Seongsu, Myeongdong, and Ikseon-dong, with stores also in
Busan, Daegu, and Suwon.

## Why it's so cheap

The pricing isn't a gimmick — it comes down to how Off Beauty buys.
Instead of going through distributors like most retailers, they buy
directly and in bulk from over 200 brands, everything from luxury
names like Sulwhasoo, Dior, and Lancôme down to trendy mid-range labels
like 3CE, Dr. Jart+, and Rom&nd. A lot of it comes through full-buyout
deals — brands clearing out packaging renewals, overproduction, or
discontinued shades — which is how a moisturizer that normally runs
₩19,000 ends up marked down to ₩3,000, or a hair dye at 93% off for
just ₩1,000. Discounts across the store generally run 20-90% off
retail.

Most of what's on the shelves still has 1-1.5 years of <a href="/vocab#yutong-gihan" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">유통기한</a>
(*yutong gihan*), shelf life, left — the steepest discounts tend to be
on the items getting closest to their date. It's genuinely earned the
nickname Korea's answer to Costco, or a "K-beauty treasure hunt," and
some industry watchers see it shaping up as a real challenger to Olive
Young. The vibe couldn't be more different, though: Off Beauty leans
into stacked, warehouse-style shelving rather than Olive Young's
curated, colorful merchandising. It looks more like a discount
grocery <a href="/vocab#aullet" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">아울렛</a>
(*aullet*), outlet, than a beauty boutique — and that's part of the
charm once you're used to it.

## What's actually worth buying

Not every category is an equally safe bet. Sheet masks, sunscreen,
color cosmetics, toners, and supplements are the categories that move
fastest and are generally considered reliable buys. Fragrance is the
one category worth being cautious about — there's been a past
authenticity issue in that category specifically, so it's not
somewhere we'd take a chance.

Whatever you're buying, check the <a href="/vocab#yutong-gihan" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">유통기한</a>
and the seal before you commit — most of it is a genuinely good deal,
but it's still worth a quick check rather than assuming everything on
the shelf is equally fresh.

there was no real reason not to try them.

## The part that makes it fun: finding the big brands

The 20-90% <a href="/vocab#seil" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">세일</a>
(*seil*) is the headline, but the real appeal is not knowing exactly
what you'll find. Inventory rotates weekly, so it's genuinely
treasure-hunt shopping — one visit might turn up a well-known luxury
name at a fraction of its normal price, and the next might not have
anything from that brand at all. That unpredictability is the whole
point: you're not going in with a shopping list, you're going in to see
what's there.

## If you're visiting as a tourist

Roughly 70% of customers at the flagship location are tourists, and
Off Beauty is set up for it — bring your <a href="/vocab#yeogwon" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">여권</a>
(*yeogwon*), passport, since you'll likely qualify for a tax refund on
top of the already-discounted price. Between the outlet pricing and the
refund, it's one of the better-value stops we've made on any recent
trip.

## Worth seeking out

If you're used to Olive Young's curated shelves, Off Beauty takes some
recalibrating — it's stacked, it's warehouse-style, and what's in stock
this week might be gone next week. But between the real discounts, the
categories that are genuinely safe buys, and the occasional big-name
find, it's earned a spot on our list of places to check every time
we're back in Korea. If you spot one you haven't been to yet, it's
worth the detour.

You don't need fluent Korean to shop confidently — on a Korean site or
in an actual store in Seoul. A small, specific set of words covers most
of what actually comes up: checking a size, asking a price, paying, and
knowing when something's out of stock. That's this list.

## The one phrase to know before any of the others

If you learn nothing else, learn <a href="/vocab#eolmayeyo" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">얼마예요</a>
(*eolmayeyo*) — "how much is it?" It works standing at a counter
pointing at something, and it works as the mental translation the
moment you see a price tag written only in Korean. Everything else on
this list is useful; this one is essential.

## Sizes and trying things on

<a href="/vocab#saijeu" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">사이즈</a>
(*saijeu*) is the word you'll see on every clothing and shoe listing,
almost always followed by a number or a letter — but Korean sizing runs
differently from US/UK/EU sizing, so the number alone won't tell you
much. If you're shopping in person, "이거 입어봐도 돼요?" (*igeo
ibeobwado dwaeyo?*) — "can I try this on?" — is the phrase that gets you
into a fitting room without any more vocabulary than that.

## Prices and discounts

Beyond 얼마예요, the other word worth knowing is <a href="/vocab#harin" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">할인</a>
(*harin*), discount — it shows up constantly in sale banners and listing
titles, usually with a percentage attached (e.g. "20% 할인" means 20%
off). On Korean shopping sites, spotting 할인 in a title is usually
faster than trying to parse the rest of the listing to find out if
something's actually on sale.

## At the checkout

<a href="/vocab#gyesan" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">계산</a>
(*gyesan*) literally means "to calculate," but in a shop it means
payment or checkout — "계산해 주세요" (*gyesanhae juseyo*) is simply
"I'd like to pay." If you want something bagged or gift-wrapped, ask for
<a href="/vocab#pojang" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">포장</a>
(*pojang*), packaging — "포장해 주세요" (*pojanghae juseyo*) covers both
a simple bag and asking for something to be gift-wrapped.

## If it's sold out

<a href="/vocab#pumjeol" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">품절</a>
(*pumjeol*) means sold out, and it's one of the most common words you'll
run into browsing Korean shopping sites — a listing marked 품절 isn't
gone for good, it's just waiting on a restock, so it's worth checking
back rather than assuming the item's discontinued.

## Quick reference

Everything from this post, in one place:

- **얼마예요** (*eolmayeyo*) — "How much is it?"
- **사이즈** (*saijeu*) — Size
- **이거 입어봐도 돼요?** (*igeo ibeobwado dwaeyo?*) — "Can I try this on?"
- **할인** (*harin*) — Discount
- **계산해 주세요** (*gyesanhae juseyo*) — "I'd like to pay" / "Check out, please"
- **포장해 주세요** (*pojanghae juseyo*) — "Please wrap/bag this"
- **품절** (*pumjeol*) — Sold out

## Worth remembering

None of this requires fluency — it's six words and two short phrases,
and together they cover checking a size, asking a price, spotting a
discount, paying, and knowing whether something's actually available.
That's most of what a shopping trip actually needs.

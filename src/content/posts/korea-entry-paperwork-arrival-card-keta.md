Before you fly to Seoul, there are two pieces of entry paperwork
worth understanding — the e-Arrival Card and K-ETA. They sound
similar, but they're not the same thing, and mixing them up is exactly
how people end up on a scam site paying way more than they should.
Here's the simple version.

## The e-Arrival Card

This is the digital replacement for the old paper arrival card you
used to fill out on the plane. As of January 1, 2026, it's the only
option — paper forms are gone.

- **Cost:** <a href="/vocab#muryo" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">무료</a>
  (*muryo*), free
- **Who needs it:** Most foreign visitors entering Korea
- **When to submit:** Within 72 hours of your arrival (it expires
  after that, so don't fill it out weeks ahead)
- **What it covers:** <a href="/vocab#yeogwon" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">여권</a>
  (*yeogwon*), passport, details, flight info, and where you're
  staying in Korea
- **Time it takes:** About 5 minutes

No printout needed — immigration pulls it up when they scan your
passport — but a screenshot of your confirmation is a smart backup.

## K-ETA

This is a separate system — a pre-travel <a href="/vocab#ipguk" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">입국</a>
(*ipguk*), entry, authorization you apply for before you even book,
not something you fill out per trip.

- **Cost:** ₩10,000 (about $8) — this one does have an official fee
- **Who needs it:** Citizens of certain visa-exempt countries —
  though currently waived through December 31, 2026 for eligible
  countries (US, UK, Canada, Australia, Japan, most of the EU, and
  others)
- **Validity:** Good for 3 years or until your passport expires —
  covers multiple trips, not just one

## How they connect

If you hold a valid K-ETA, it covers your arrival declaration too, so
you skip the e-Arrival Card. If you don't have one — including if
you're using the current exemption — you'll need to submit the
e-Arrival Card instead. Right now, most exempt-country travelers fall
into this second group: no K-ETA needed, but the e-Arrival Card still
is.

## Avoid third-party sites

Search "Korea arrival card" and you'll likely see paid third-party
sites before the real one. Some charge anywhere from $10 to well over
$100 for a form that's free, or for K-ETA processing well above the
actual $8 fee. They're not stealing your info — they typically do
submit the form — you're just paying for something you could do
yourself in minutes. Still, it's the same pattern as any <a href="/vocab#sagi" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">사기</a>
(*sagi*), scam, site: markup on something that should be free or
cheap, dressed up to look official.

Only use the official government sites:

- **e-Arrival Card:** e-arrivalcard.go.kr
- **K-ETA:** k-eta.go.kr

Before entering any passport information, check the URL. If it
doesn't end in `.go.kr`, close the tab.

Every country has food trends. But South Korea doesn't just have trends — it
has *events*. A snack shows up, an entire nation loses its mind over it,
lines form around the block, and then, almost as fast as it arrived, it's
gone. Thanks to social media, the rest of us get a front-row seat to the
chaos. Here's a decade of Korea's yummiest obsessions, one year at a time.

## Early 2010s: The bubble tea boom

Before it was a global staple, <a href="/vocab#beobeulti" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">버블티</a>
(*beobeulti*) and Gong Cha (공차) swept through Korea and never really left.
Around the same time, sweet crispy fried chicken bites
(<a href="/vocab#dakgangjeong" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">닭강정</a>)
and Sulbing-style shaved ice (설빙/빙수) turned "dessert café" into a full
lifestyle. This is the era that basically invented the Korean café-hopping
culture you see all over Instagram today.

## 2017: The corn dog comeback

<a href="/vocab#myeongnang-hatdogeu" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">명랑핫도그</a>
(*Myeongrang Hotdog*) took the humble corn dog, rolled it in sugar, and
somehow made it a street food icon. Suddenly every market and shopping
street had a stand selling them, and "sugar-dusted corn dog" became a
certified national craving.

## 2019: The year of spice and crunch

This one's a triple threat.
<a href="/vocab#malatang" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">마라탕</a>
(*mala tang*), mala xiang guo, and hot pot (마라샹궈 / 훠궈) landed hard, and
the tongue-numbing Sichuan spice took over group dinners everywhere. At the
same time, the
<a href="/vocab#keuropeul" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">크로플</a>
(*croffle*) — a croissant pressed and cooked like a waffle — became the
café menu item you couldn't escape. And on every highway rest stop, people
were suddenly obsessed with chewy Chinese glass noodles (중국당면). Big
year. Very different flavors, all equally unstoppable.

## 2020: Dalgona takes over the world

If you were online in 2020, you already know.
<a href="/vocab#dalgona-keopi" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">달고나커피</a>
(*dalgona keopi*) — instant coffee whipped with sugar and water until it
turned into a cloud-like foam — became the defining lockdown-era trend, not
just in Korea but globally. Everyone had a whisk, everyone had an opinion
on how long to whip it, and everyone's feed looked the same for a solid few
months.

## 2021: Pink sauce, big feelings

Rosé sauce swept the nation.
<a href="/vocab#roje-tteokbokki" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">로제떡볶이</a>
(*roje tteokbokki*) and rosé pasta (로제파스타) took the familiar
spicy-sweet tteokbokki base and mellowed it out with cream, and suddenly
everyone who "couldn't handle spicy food" had a seat at the table. Pretty,
pink, and everywhere.

## 2022: The sticker economy

Technically this was a bread trend. Realistically, nobody cared about the
bread.
<a href="/vocab#poketmon-ppang" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">포켓몬빵</a>
(*Pokémon bread*) came back on shelves with collectible stickers
(<a href="/vocab#ttibu-ssil" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">띠부씰</a>)
inside, and the resulting frenzy turned convenience stores into hunting
grounds. Kids and adults alike were buying loaves just to open them, grab
the sticker, and toss the bread. Iconic.

## 2023: Candy-coated chaos

<a href="/vocab#tanghuru" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">탕후루</a>
(*tanghulu*) — Chinese-style fruit skewers dipped in a hard candy shell —
became the single biggest food story of the year. One franchise reportedly
went from about 50 stores to over 300 in just five months. Teenagers
walking around with a stick of glossy, red-coated strawberries became one
of the defining street images of 2023.

## 2024: Dubai chocolate fever

The pistachio-cream, crispy-kadayif Dubai chocolate bar went viral
worldwide, and Korea caught the fever just as hard as everywhere else.
Cafés scrambled to make their own versions, TikTok and Instagram were
flooded with ASMR chocolate-cracking videos, and pistachio cream briefly
became the most requested flavor in the country.

## 2025–2026: The Dubai chocolate sequel nobody saw coming

Just when Dubai chocolate started to fade, Korea did what Korea does best —
remixed it. Enter
<a href="/vocab#dujjonku" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">두쫀쿠</a>
(*Dubai jjonduk-kuki*), a chewy Korean cookie spin on the same
pistachio-cream flavor profile. A whole new wave of "Dubai" flavored
everything followed it into 2026.

## Why does this keep happening?

Short-form video is basically rocket fuel for food trends. A trend used to
take months to build and fade — now some of them peak and disappear within
a couple of weeks. Croffle took about 163 days to lose half its search
buzz back in 2020; tanghulu did it in 54 days in 2023; Dubai chocolate did
it in just 13. The trend cycle isn't just fast anymore — it's compressing
every single year.

So the next time you see a Korean food suddenly flooding your feed, don't
wait too long to try it. By the time you've made a reservation, the whole
country might have already moved on to the next one.

Seoul is one of those cities that manages to feel like two places at
once — centuries-old palaces sitting a few subway stops from glass
skyscrapers and rooftop cafes. Four days isn't enough to see
everything, but it's enough to get a real feel for the city if you
plan it right. Here's a day-by-day route that balances history, food,
views, and modern Seoul without doubling back across the city.

## Day 1: Palaces and old Seoul

**Morning — Gyeongbokgung Palace.** Start at the largest and most
iconic of Seoul's Five Grand <a href="/vocab#gunggwol" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">궁궐</a>
(*gunggwol*). Try to arrive by 9–10am to beat the crowds and catch the
<a href="/vocab#sumunjang-gyodaesik" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">수문장 교대식</a>
(*sumunjang gyodaesik*), the Changing of the Guard ceremony, held twice
daily at 10am and 2pm. Closed on Tuesdays, so plan around that if your
trip lands on one.

<img src="/images/posts/gyeongbokgung-palace.png" alt="Geunjeongjeon, the main throne hall of Gyeongbokgung Palace, on a clear morning" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

**Late morning — Bukchon Hanok Village.** A short walk from the
palace, this traditional <a href="/vocab#hanok" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">한옥</a>
(*hanok*) neighborhood is best experienced early — 30–45 minutes
before 9am if you can manage it, since the alleys go from quiet and
atmospheric to crowded fast once tour groups arrive. Even arriving
mid-morning still gets you photo-worthy rooftop views and a sense of
what old Seoul looked like.

<img src="/images/posts/bukchon-hanok-village-alley.png" alt="A quiet hanok-lined alley in Bukchon Hanok Village at sunrise" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

**Afternoon — Myeongdong.** Chaotic in the best way — street food
stalls, K-pop blasting from storefronts, and more skincare shops than
you'll know what to do with. Grab tteokbokki (spicy rice cakes),
hotteok (sweet filled pancakes), and skewered fried chicken as you
wander. This is also a good spot to pick up a few of the Olive Young
products from our <a href="/blog/glass-skin-101-routine-breakdown" class="text-brand-700 underline">Glass Skin 101 routine</a>
if you didn't order online ahead of time.

<img src="/images/posts/myeongdong-street-food-night.png" alt="Street food carts and crowds along a Myeongdong shopping street at dusk" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

## Day 2: Trendy neighborhoods

**Morning — Seongsu-dong.** Known as "the Brooklyn of Seoul," this is
the neighborhood for creative cafes, converted warehouse spaces,
street art, and small design shops. It's a slower, more local pace
than Day 1 — good for wandering without an itinerary.

<img src="/images/posts/seongsu-dong-cafe-street.png" alt="A quiet Seongsu-dong street lined with converted brick warehouse cafes and shops" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

**Afternoon — back to Myeongdong, or a second district.** If you
didn't finish exploring Myeongdong, use the afternoon to close it out,
or head to a second trendy area depending on your interests (Hongdae
for a younger, artsy energy; Garosugil for a more upscale shopping
stretch).

## Day 3: Views and markets

**Late afternoon/evening — N Seoul Tower.** Time this for sunset or
after dark — the panoramic <a href="/vocab#yagyeong" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">야경</a>
(*yagyeong*) over the city is the main draw, and Seoul's skyline
lights up in a way that's worth planning your schedule around.

<img src="/images/posts/n-seoul-tower-night.png" alt="N Seoul Tower lit up at night on Namsan mountain, overlooking the city" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

**Evening — Mangwon Market.** Skip Gwangjang — it's gotten a rough
reputation in recent years for tourist-inflated prices — and head to
Mangwon instead, a <a href="/vocab#jaerae-sijang" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">재래시장</a>
(*jaerae sijang*) in Mapo-gu that's stayed popular with actual locals
rather than tour groups. Graze on hotteok, twigim (fried snacks), and
tteokbokki at prices that still feel like a neighborhood market, then
walk it off along the Han River at nearby Mangwon Hangang Park.

## Day 4: Modern Seoul (Gangnam)

**Morning/afternoon — Lotte World Mall and Seoul Sky.** Cross the Han
River into Gangnam for a completely different side of the city. Lotte
World Mall combines luxury shopping, the Lotte World Aquarium, and the
Seoul Sky Observatory — sitting 555 meters up on the 123rd floor, it's
one of the best views in the city, weather permitting.

<img src="/images/posts/lotte-world-tower-seoul.png" alt="Lotte World Tower rising over the Seokchon Lake waterfront at dusk" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

**Afternoon — COEX Mall and Starfield Library.** Nearby, the Starfield
Library inside COEX Mall has become one of Seoul's most photographed
spots — floor-to-ceiling bookshelves under a soaring atrium, free to
visit and worth the short stop even if you're not there to read.

<img src="/images/posts/starfield-library-seoul.png" alt="The soaring bookshelves and reading tables of Starfield Library inside COEX Mall" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

## Practical tips

- **Get the Climate Card.** New for 2026, this tourist transit pass is
  worth picking up on arrival — it simplifies paying for subway and
  bus rides across the city and is a noticeable upgrade from juggling
  single tickets. See our
  <a href="/blog/t-money-vs-climate-card" class="text-brand-700 underline">T-money Card vs. Climate Card breakdown</a>
  for which one actually fits your trip.
- **Wear real walking shoes.** This itinerary (like most Seoul
  itineraries) is walking-heavy, especially Days 1 and 3.
- **Check palace closures before you go.** Most major palaces close
  one day a week (often Tuesday), so it's worth confirming the current
  schedule before building your morning around one.

## If you have more time

Four days only scratches the surface. If your trip allows for it,
Seoul pairs well with a short trip outside the city — Gyeongju for
history or <a href="/blog/gamcheon-village-busan" class="text-brand-700 underline">Busan</a>
for coastal food and a change of pace are both reachable by express
bus or KTX and worth considering as an extension rather than trying to
cram more into Seoul itself.

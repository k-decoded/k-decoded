If you've noticed Seoul travel content suddenly full of people holding
up colored fabric swatches under their chin, you're not imagining a
niche trend — personal color analysis has become one of the most
searched first-timer activities for visitors planning a Seoul trip,
right up there with palace visits and skincare shopping.

## What it actually is

<a href="/vocab#peoseoneol-keolleo" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">퍼스널 컬러</a>
(*peoseoneol keolleo*), personal color, is a consultation that figures
out your natural undertone and hands you a palette of colors —
clothing, makeup, hair — that are scientifically flattering against
your specific skin, rather than colors that are just generally
trendy. It's not a new idea (the concept has roots in Western color
theory going back decades), but Korea has turned it into a
genuinely polished, science-backed service industry of its own.

## What happens in a session

A typical <a href="/vocab#jindan" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">진단</a>
(*jindan*), diagnosis, runs 60–90 minutes and moves through a few
stages:

1. **A skin reading.** Many studios start with a spectrophotometer —
   a device that objectively measures redness, yellowness, and
   brightness in your skin, rather than relying purely on the
   consultant's eye.
2. **Draping.** You sit in front of a mirror under daylight-balanced
   lighting while the consultant holds a sequence of colored fabric
   drapes under your chin, watching how each one changes your
   complexion — some colors will visibly brighten and smooth your
   skin, others will wash you out or make you look tired.
3. **A pouch check.** Bring your current makeup bag if you can — a
   lot of studios will sort your existing products into "best,"
   "worst," and "okay" for your new palette.
4. **Results.** You walk away with a palette (physical swatches or a
   digital card), plus recommendations for hair color and jewelry
   tone (gold vs. silver).

## The season system

Korean personal color analysis is built on a <a href="/vocab#sagyejeol" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">사계절</a>
(*sagyejeol*), four-season framework — Spring, Summer, Autumn, and
Winter — split along two axes: <a href="/vocab#womton" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">웜톤</a>
(*womton*) vs. <a href="/vocab#kulton" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">쿨톤</a>
(*kulton*), warm tone vs. cool tone, and bright vs. muted. Spring and
Autumn are warm; Summer and Winter are cool — and most people find the
warm/cool split alone is the single most useful takeaway from the
whole session, even before you get into which season you are
specifically.

Standard sessions stop at the four seasons. Premium sessions go
further, splitting each season into 12 or even 16 sub-types (adding
light, medium, and deep variations within each), which is where the
price starts climbing.

## What it costs

Pricing varies by session type and studio, but the ranges have
settled into a fairly consistent pattern in 2026:

| Session type | Length | Price (KRW) | Price (USD) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group (2–3 people) | 30–40 min | ₩60,000–120,000/person | ~$45–90 |
| Standard 1:1 | 60–90 min | ₩100,000–180,000 | ~$75–135 |
| English-speaking | 60–90 min | ₩120,000–180,000 | ~$90–135 |
| Premium (12/16-tone) | 90–120 min | ₩250,000–400,000+ | ~$190–300+ |

English-speaking sessions sit at the higher end mostly because fewer
studios offer them, not because the analysis itself is more involved.

## Booking tips

- **Book through Klook, Creatrip, or Trazy rather than direct** —
  these platforms often run 10–15% cheaper than booking with the
  studio directly, on top of being easier to book in English.
- **Show up bare-faced.** Makeup interferes with the skin reading and
  the draping results — most studios will explicitly ask you not to
  wear any.
- **Bring your actual makeup bag**, not just the products you think
  are your "best" ones — the pouch check is more useful with your full
  real-world rotation.

## The free DIY version

If you want a rough read before committing to a paid session — or
you're not able to fit one into your trip — there are a few
low-effort self-tests:

<img src="/images/posts/personal-color-analysis-diy-jewelry-test.png" alt="A woman holding a gold hoop earring and a silver hoop earring up to her face in front of a mirror, with a white sheet of paper on the table for comparison" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

- **Gold vs. silver jewelry.** Hold both against your face in natural
  light. Gold flattering you more suggests warm; silver suggests cool.
- **White paper test.** Hold a plain white sheet next to your cheek.
  If your skin looks sallow or yellow-green against it, you likely
  lean warm; if it looks pink or ruddy, you likely lean cool.
- **Coral vs. blue-pink lipstick.** Try both against bare lips —
  whichever looks more natural rather than harsh is a decent
  undertone clue.

None of this replaces a real diagnosis, but it's a reasonable way to
walk in with a guess already in mind — or to decide the paid session
isn't necessary for what you need.

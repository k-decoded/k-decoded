Korean food is far more than sizzling BBQ and spicy noodles. This is a growing collection of beloved classics and little discoveries from our food adventures—one delicious bite at a time.

## 떡 (Tteok) — Korean rice cakes

<img src="/images/posts/korean-tteok-market.jpg" alt="Colourful varieties of Korean tteok, or rice cakes, arranged at a market stall" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

Soft, chewy and incredibly colourful, <a href="/vocab#tteok" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">떡</a> (*tteok*) is a traditional Korean snack made from rice. You will find it in endless shapes and flavours at markets, bakeries and celebrations.

- The rice cake itself can taste slightly savoury, but it still works beautifully as a sweet treat.
- Look out for fillings such as honey, sweet red bean or nutty sesame.
- I genuinely loved tteok and was always on the lookout for it while wandering Korean markets.
- My favourite? The ones with a sweet honey filling inside. Highly recommend!

**Tiny tip:** buy a few different colours and shapes when you spot a stall—half the fun is finding your favourite.

## 호두과자 (Hodu-gwaja) — Walnut pastries

<img src="/images/posts/korean-hodu-gwaja.jpg" alt="Freshly baked Korean hodu-gwaja walnut pastries being held in front of their box" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

These little walnut-shaped pastries are small, warm and absolutely delicious. Hodu-gwaja usually have a soft cake-like shell with sweet red bean paste and little walnut pieces tucked inside.

- You can find them almost anywhere—especially inside stations and underground malls.
- They are inexpensive, and you usually get a generous little bagful.
- Best eaten warm on a super-cold day. Instant comfort snack.

**Tiny tip:** follow the smell of freshly baked pastry. It is almost impossible to walk past without buying some.

## 떡볶이 (Tteokbokki) — Spicy rice cakes

<img src="/images/posts/korean-tteokbokki-street-stall.jpg" alt="A bubbling pan of Korean tteokbokki with rice cakes, eggs and fish cakes at a street-food stall" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

The face of Korean street food: <a href="/vocab#tteokbokki" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">떡볶이</a> (*tteokbokki*). Chewy rice cakes swimming in a sweet, spicy red sauce—easily one of my top three Korean food cravings.

    <figcaption class="mt-2 text-center text-xs text-gray-400">Street-stall tteokbokki in the making</figcaption>

- The sauce is sweet first, spicy second—and wonderfully addictive.
- It is brilliant with fish cakes, boiled eggs, fried snacks or a quick dip of <a href="/vocab#gimbap" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">김밥</a> (*gimbap*).
- My go-to order is tteokbokki with <a href="/vocab#sundae" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">순대</a> (*sundae*, Korean blood sausage) or mini gimbap.

**Tiny tip:** do not leave that glossy sauce behind—use it as a dip for everything on your tray.

## 번데기 (Beondegi) — Silkworm pupae

<img src="/images/posts/korean-beondegi-stall.jpg" alt="A Korean street-food vendor serving beondegi from a steaming pot" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

<a href="/vocab#beondegi" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">번데기</a> (*beondegi*) literally means “pupa”—specifically silkworm pupae, boiled or steamed and sold as a traditional street snack. Scary? A little. Worth trying? Honestly, yes.

<figure class="not-prose my-8">
  <img src="/images/posts/korean-beondegi-cup.jpg" alt="A paper cup filled with cooked Korean beondegi, or silkworm pupae" class="w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />
  <figcaption class="mt-2 text-center text-xs text-gray-400">A cup of beondegi for the brave</figcaption>
</figure>

- I was influenced by my husband, who would not stop talking about the protein.
- Once you get past what it is, the flavour is an interesting mix of sweet, salty and nutty.
- To be honest… it was not that bad!

**Tiny tip:** start with one. If you like it, congratulations—you have unlocked a proper Korean street-food adventure.

## 치킨 (Chikin) — Korean fried chicken

<img src="/images/posts/korean-fried-chicken.jpg" alt="A table spread of Korean fried chicken in several flavours, including glazed and cheese-topped pieces" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

How could I not mention <a href="/vocab#chikin" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">치킨</a> (*chikin*)—the epitome of fried chicken? I may be a little biased, but Korean fried chicken is hands down some of the best.

- Korea has somehow perfected that super-crispy outer layer while keeping the chicken juicy inside.
- The best part is choosing a flavour: sweet and spicy, soy garlic, honey butter, classic crispy—and plenty more.
- My personal favourite is snow cheese: crunchy chicken covered in a fluffy, savoury cheese powder. So good.

**Tiny tip:** order a half-and-half platter if you cannot pick one flavour. You probably will not be able to.

## 간장게장 (Ganjang-gejang) — Soy sauce marinated crab

<img src="/images/posts/korean-ganjang-gejang.jpg" alt="Soy-marinated crab and spicy marinated crab served with Korean side dishes" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

Oh boy, this is good stuff—especially if you are a seafood fan. <a href="/vocab#ganjang-gejang" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">간장게장</a> (*ganjang-gejang*) is raw crab marinated in soy sauce, and its rich, savoury marinade is every bit as good as the crab itself.

<figure class="not-prose my-8">
  <img src="/images/posts/korean-ganjang-gejang-serving.jpg" alt="Soy sauce marinated crab being served at a Korean restaurant" class="w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />
  <figcaption class="mt-2 text-center text-xs text-gray-400">Cracking into the good stuff</figcaption>
</figure>

- We tried both the original soy sauce version and <a href="/vocab#yangnyeom-gejang" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">양념게장</a> (*yangnyeom-gejang*), the spicy-sweet seasoned version.
- They are completely different, but neither loses on flavour.
- The crab meat is soft, rich and practically melt-in-your-mouth.
- It pairs ridiculously well with a bowl of hot, steamy rice.

**Tiny tip:** if raw crab is your thing, do not skip this. Spoon some marinade over your rice, too—it is the whole experience.

## 쫀드기 (Jjondeugi) — Chewy corn snack

<img src="/images/posts/korean-jjondeugi-stall.jpg" alt="A Korean street-food stall selling cups of seasoned jjondeugi snack sticks" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

<a href="/vocab#jjondeugi" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">쫀드기</a> (*jjondeugi*) was completely new to me until my husband introduced it—and oh boy, am I glad he did. This nostalgic street snack is made from corn starch and is all about its satisfyingly chewy texture.

<figure class="not-prose my-8">
  <img src="/images/posts/korean-jjondeugi-snack.jpg" alt="A paper cup filled with seasoned Korean jjondeugi snack sticks" class="w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />
  <figcaption class="mt-2 text-center text-xs text-gray-400">A little cup of chewy, seasoned goodness</figcaption>
</figure>

- Jjondeugi has been a popular Korean snack for decades—and once you try it, you get it.
- We had hot chilli and Prinkle flavours.
- Prinkle, a sweet-and-savoury cheesy seasoning, was definitely my favourite.

**Tiny tip:** do not judge it by its simple look. One bite turns into another handful very quickly.

## 빙수 (Bingsu) — Korean shaved ice

<img src="/images/posts/korean-bingsu.jpg" alt="Korean bingsu shaved ice topped with strawberries, cream, injeolmi powder and almonds" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

<a href="/vocab#bingsu" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">빙수</a> (*bingsu*) is Korean shaved ice—and it is the perfect cool-down dessert. Compared with the lighter, water-ice style you may know from Japanese kakigori, bingsu is often made with frozen milk, so it feels extra soft, creamy and fluffy.

- It is a staple during Korea's hotter seasons, when an icy bowl of bingsu is exactly what you need.
- Sulbing is the go-to bingsu café chain, with plenty of tempting flavours and toppings to choose from.
- Toppings can go in every direction: fruit, condensed milk, red beans, mochi, cheesecake and more.
- The strawberry version is bright, fresh and very hard to share.
- I always end up getting injeolmi bingsu. Topped with roasted soybean powder and chewy rice cake, it is nutty, cosy and perfect for anyone else who is a massive sucker for soybean flavour.

**Tiny tip:** do not wait for summer. Bingsu is good whenever you need an excuse for dessert.

## 칼국수 (Kalguksu) — Knife-cut noodles

<img src="/images/posts/korean-kalguksu.jpg" alt="A bowl of Korean kalguksu with hand-cut noodles in a creamy white broth, served with kimchi and dumplings" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

I LOVE <a href="/vocab#kalguksu" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">칼국수</a> (*kalguksu*). Hand-cut noodles in a warm broth—what could go wrong? This is classic Korean comfort food at its best.

- The broad, handmade noodles are soft, satisfying and perfect for soaking up the broth.
- I usually choose the white, milky-looking broth because it feels so hearty and warming.
- The flavour is clean, comforting and an absolute must for colder seasons.

**Tiny tip:** add kimchi on the side for a spicy little contrast to the soothing broth.

## 감자탕 (Gamjatang) — Spicy pork-bone soup

<img src="/images/posts/korean-gamjatang.jpg" alt="A large bowl of Korean gamjatang spicy pork-bone soup with potatoes and greens, served with side dishes" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

<a href="/vocab#gamjatang" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">감자탕</a> (*gamjatang*) is a rich, spicy soup made with pork spine or neck bones, potatoes and plenty of greens. It is hearty in every possible way.

- We had an incredible bowl at a restaurant in Seongsu—chef’s kiss, even if I cannot remember the name for the life of me.
- The portions were seriously generous. We definitely got our money’s worth without breaking the bank.
- The meat fell straight off the bone: super soft, tender and worth every bite.
- The hearty soup was packed with vegetables and, of course, plenty of potatoes.

**Tiny tip:** arrive hungry. Gamjatang does not believe in small portions.

## 카페 (Kape) — Korean coffee-chain drinks

<img src="/images/posts/korean-coffee-chain-banana-milk.jpg" alt="A banana milk coffee from a Korean coffee chain, held outside the café" class="not-prose my-8 w-full rounded-xl object-cover" />

Korean <a href="/vocab#kape" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">카페</a> (*kape*) chains are one of my favourite little travel treats: the drinks are big, affordable and genuinely delicious.

- I make it a mission to try something new whenever I spot a menu.
- This banana-milk coffee was the crème de la crème for me—creamy, sweet and coffee-forward in the best way.
- From fruit ades to playful lattes and seasonal drinks, have a look at the menu and see what interesting flavour you find.

**Tiny tip:** do not automatically order your usual. Korean coffee chains are made for trying something unexpected.

## Until the next bite

That was only the tip of the iceberg when it comes to Korean food. Korea has so much fantastic cuisine to explore and discover—I just wish I had the stomach space for it all!

I hope this post has shared even a small insight into the wonderful world of Korean food, and inspires you to venture out and try something new.

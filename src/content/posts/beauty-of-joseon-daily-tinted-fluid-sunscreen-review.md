I picked up Beauty of Joseon’s **Daily Tinted Fluid Sunscreen** at Olive Young because I loved the idea of a light base product that could do a little more than SPF alone. It gives sheer coverage and a healthy sheen, but the shade selection is the first thing I would flag.

## The shade-range reality

The range at Olive Young felt very slim. I chose the darkest shade I could find, and it was still a little light for my skin. The coverage is light enough that it does not look dramatically off once blended, but I needed contour and blush to bring warmth and dimension back into the face.

That makes this feel more like a flexible, tinted <a href="/vocab#jawoeseon-chadanje" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">자외선 차단제</a> (*jawoeseon chadanje*), or sunscreen, than a complexion product meant to match precisely. If you like a very sheer base, it may be workable; if your skin tone sits beyond the available range, test it in person first.

## What the formula is designed to do

Beauty of Joseon describes this as a 50 mL, lightweight mineral tinted sunscreen with sheer coverage. The listed UV filter is 20.90% zinc oxide, while mineral pigments (iron oxides) create the tint. The formula also includes silica for a less shiny feel, olive-derived lipids, and *Artemisia capillaris* extract.

The brand lists 12 flexible shades online, although the selection I found at Olive Young was much smaller in practice. My tube states **SPF50+ PA++++**; Beauty of Joseon’s regional product pages currently show different SPF ratings, so check the packaging and the official listing for your market when you buy.

## Texture and finish

The formula is genuinely light and fluid. It spreads easily and gives a little evening-out effect — the kind of subtle <a href="/vocab#tone-up" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">톤업</a> (*tone up*), or complexion brightening, that lets skin still look like skin rather than covering everything up.

It does leave a slightly greasy finish on me, especially at first. That is easy to adjust: I go over it with loose or pressed powder wherever I want a more matte, <a href="/vocab#bosongbosong" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">보송보송</a> (*bosongbosong*), soft and non-shiny, finish. With that extra step, it sits much more comfortably for my preferred makeup look.

## For warmer days

For me, it is a real plus in hotter weather. It has the lightweight wear I want in summer, while the formula still has enough <a href="/vocab#milchakryeok" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">밀착력</a> (*milchakryeok*), meaning how well it adheres to the skin, to feel dependable through the day. It wore well for me in warm conditions, although I could not find a verified official water-resistance claim, so I would still reapply sunscreen as needed.

Because it is a mineral formula — <a href="/vocab#mugi-jacha" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">무기자차</a> (*mugi jacha*), Korean skincare shorthand for mineral sunscreen — I make sure to shake it well and apply an even amount as the final step of my routine. Beauty of Joseon also recommends shaking before use, then blending with fingers, a brush, or a sponge.

## Verdict

I like this as a light, summer-friendly SPF base, not as a replacement for foundation. The fluid texture is comfortable, the tint is easy to make work with a little contour and blush, and powder solves the greasier finish for me. My biggest hesitation is still the limited shade range: even the deepest option I found at Olive Young was lighter than I wanted.

If your shade is available, this is a good pick for minimal-makeup days. If not, the formula may be lovely, but the match may take more work than it should.

For 25 minutes on November 19, Korea will adjust aircraft takeoffs and
landings so students can hear their English listening test. Earlier
that morning, the Seoul area will run extra subway trains to get them
to their exam sites. The same test is also the reason someone might
give a teenager a box of sticky rice cakes. [Ministry of Education exam-day plan](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148973935)

## What Suneung is and when it happens

**수능** (*suneung*) is short for 대학수학능력시험, the College
Scholastic Ability Test. Its scores are used in university admissions.
This sitting is on **Thursday, November 19, 2026**, with about **552,000
candidates** expected. The official name uses **2027학년도** because
the test is for the 2027 academic year. Scores are due on **December
11**. [Exam briefing](https://www.korea.kr/briefing/policyBriefingView.do?newsId=156752001) · [Candidate figures](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148973935)

## What changes on exam day

- **Morning commute.** The government asked public offices and businesses
  to start at **10 a.m. or later**. The Seoul metropolitan
  area plans extra subway services between **6 a.m. and 8:10 a.m.**
- **Near exam sites.** Vehicle access is restricted within **200 metres**
  of test sites. Allow extra time if a taxi route passes a school being
  used for the test.
- **English listening.** Aircraft takeoffs and landings are adjusted
  between **1:10 and 1:35 p.m.** If you're flying that day, check your
  airline's latest timing. [Ministry of Education exam-day plan](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148973935)

## Why the good-luck gifts are sticky

**붙다** (*butda*) means “to stick.” In **시험에 붙다** it means “to
pass an exam.” That double meaning explains two classic gifts for a
**수험생** (*suheomsaeng*, exam candidate). **엿** (*yeot*) is Korean
taffy, and **찹쌀떡** (*chapssaltteok*) is the sticky glutinous rice
cake we covered in our [tteok pilgrimage guide](/blog/tteok-pilgrimage-korea-rice-cake-dessert-trend).
Both let you wish someone luck with a snack. [Korea.net on exam gifts](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=143682) · [The Korea Herald on the wordplay](https://m.koreaherald.com/article/3853011)

If you're texting someone before the test, **시험 잘 봐!** (*Siheom
jal bwa!*) is a friendly “Good luck on your exam!” Use **시험 잘
보세요!** (*Siheom jal boseyo!*) when you want to be more polite.

If a translucent cherry-red brush with an oversized bow has been floating
around your feed, it's not fan art — it's a real product, and it's
reportedly sold through a 300-unit daily allocation at a Korean pop-up
every single day it's been open. IVE's Jang Wonyoung has turned a
hairbrush into one of this year's more unlikely K-beauty hits. Here's
what it actually is, why it's selling the way it is, and whether the
four-layer design underneath the branding holds up.

## What is the Wonyoung cherry brush?

Its full name is the FOREVER:CHERRY x Medicube Ribbon Cherry Glass Hair
Brush — FOREVER:CHERRY being the "worldview" project built around Jang
Wonyoung, and this brush is its first official product. It launched in
Japan on May 11, 2026, at ¥2,970 (about $17), before expanding to China
and, by July, a Korean duty-free push through Shinsegae. The two motifs
in the name aren't just decoration: a cherry-shaped, semi-translucent
pink body, and a three-dimensional ribbon bow that doubles as the grip.

## Why it actually blew up

It's tempting to chalk this up to "Wonyoung fans will buy anything," but
the sales numbers are real on their own terms: the brush hit #1 overall
on Qoo10 Japan on launch day, and by July had passed 100,000 cumulative
units sold across Japan and China in roughly two months. A Lotte
Department Store pop-up in Korea reportedly <a href="/vocab#wanpan" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">완판</a>
(*wanpan*) — sold out — its 300-brush daily allocation every day it ran.

A few things are stacking on top of each other here:

- **The Wonyoung effect.** She's one of the most recognized Korean-wave
  figures in Japan specifically, and a ¥2,970 brush is a far easier
  buy-in to her aesthetic than anything in fashion or luxury beauty.
- **It's built for social feeds.** The translucent cherry body and
  oversized bow read instantly in a photo, which matters as much as the
  bristles for something this shareable.
- **The price is low for idol merch.** Fans online were reportedly
  surprised it wasn't priced like typical celebrity merchandise —
  cheaper, in fact, than plenty of ordinary phone grips.
- **There's a real product underneath the branding**, which is the part
  most coverage skips past.

## The one genuinely interesting part: four bristle lengths

Medicube markets the brush around a four-tier pin structure, with each
layer doing a different job as it passes through hair:

| Layer | Job |
| --- | --- |
| Long | Reaches deepest, meets <a href="/vocab#eongkim" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">엉킴</a> (*eongkim*, tangles) first |
| Medium | Aligns hair flow |
| Short | Smooths the cuticle surface |
| Micro | Gathers and finishes the finest strands |

Rather than every bristle hitting a knot at the same depth, the graduated
lengths are meant to work through hair progressively — which is also the
standard design logic behind detangling brushes generally, cherry
branding aside. The base sits on a cushioned four-layer pad, which
Medicube specifically promotes as reducing pressure on the <a href="/vocab#dupi" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">두피</a>
(*dupi*), scalp, while brushing.

## What it's actually supposed to do

Medicube's marketing leans hard on <a href="/vocab#yuri-meoritgyeol" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">유리 머릿결</a>
(*yuri meoritgyeol*), "glass hair" — but it's worth separating the
plausible mechanism from the marketing language. A brush can't add gloss
to hair. What graduated, cushioned bristles can reasonably do is
detangle with less pulling, align strands more evenly, and smooth the
surface — and hair that's smoother and less roughed-up genuinely does
reflect light more evenly, which reads as shinier. That's a real effect;
it's just friction reduction, not a coating.

## Is it actually worth it?

Here's the honest answer: as a hairbrush, it's a solid, fairly ordinary
detangler — reviewers who've compared it side by side describe it as
functionally close to a Tangle Teezer. What you're really paying the
~$17 for is the ribbon-and-cherry design, the storage stand and
protective cap, and the fact that it's the first product to carry
Wonyoung's own FOREVER:CHERRY branding rather than a generic endorsement
deal. If you want a detangling brush and don't care about any of that,
a cheaper no-name four-layer brush will do the same job. If part of what
you're buying is a wearable piece of the FOREVER:CHERRY aesthetic at a
non-luxury price, this is a well-made way to do that — and the resale
and sellout numbers suggest a lot of people have already decided it's
worth it on those terms.

## Bottom line

The Medicube Cherry Brush works as both a beauty tool and a K-pop
collectible because it doesn't fully fake being either one — the
four-layer bristle design is a legitimate, if unremarkable, detangling
brush, and the cherry-and-ribbon styling is distinctive enough that
someone scrolling past genuinely stops to ask what it is. That
combination, more than the Wonyoung name alone, is what turned a $17
hairbrush into a 100,000-unit seller in two months.

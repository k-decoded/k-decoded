If you've ever picked up a Korean skincare product and felt a little lost
between the Hangul and the Konglish, you're not alone. Korean beauty labels
use a specific vocabulary — some borrowed from English, some purely Korean —
that shows up again and again once you know what to look for. Here are 10
terms worth learning so you can shop with confidence.

## 1. <a href="/vocab#esenseu" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">에센스</a> (*esenseu*) — essence

Not the same as a serum, even though Western brands often use the words
interchangeably. A Korean essence is lighter, sits between toner and serum in
a routine, and is meant to boost hydration and help the rest of your routine
absorb better.

## 2. <a href="/vocab#aempul" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">앰플</a> (*aempul*) — ampoule

A concentrated, potent version of a serum — usually used short-term or for a
specific concern (brightening, calming, etc.) rather than as a daily staple.
Think of it as the "treatment" step.

![A dropper releasing a golden ampoule onto a fingertip](/images/posts/korean-skincare-ampoule-dropper.png)

## 3. <a href="/vocab#jinjeong" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">진정</a> (*jinjeong*) — "soothing" / "calming"

You'll see this a lot on products meant to calm redness or irritation. If a
label says <a href="/vocab#jinjeong" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">진정</a>,
it's signaling the product is gentle and calming-focused, often centered on
ingredients like centella or aloe.

## 4. <a href="/vocab#subun" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">수분</a> (*subun*) — "moisture" / "hydration"

A very common word for water-based hydration, as opposed to
<span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">영양</span> (*yeongyang*,
nutrition/nourishment), which usually signals richer, more emollient
formulas.

## 5. <a href="/vocab#mibaek" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">미백</a> (*mibaek*) — "brightening"

Despite the literal translation leaning toward "whitening," in modern K-beauty
marketing this almost always means brightening/evening tone — not skin
lightening in the way the literal translation might suggest to English
speakers.

## 6. <a href="/vocab#tallyeok" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">탄력</a> (*tallyeok*) — "elasticity" / "firmness"

Common on anti-aging or firming products. If you see this alongside collagen
or retinol-adjacent ingredients, it's an anti-aging-focused product.

## 7. <a href="/vocab#jeojageuk" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">저자극</a> (*jeojageuk*) — "low irritation" / "gentle"

A reassurance label similar to "hypoallergenic" in Western marketing — often
paired with sensitive-skin lines.

## 8. <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">크림</span> (*keurim*, cream) vs <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">로션</span> (*losyeon*, lotion)

Straightforward, but worth knowing the distinction holds in Korean routines
too — lotion is lighter and more fluid, cream is heavier and used as a
sealing step.

## 9. <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">팩</span> (*paek*) — pack

This refers to masks — but "sheet mask" specifically is usually
<span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">시트팩</span> (*siteupaek*),
while a wash-off clay or gel mask is often just called 팩 or
<span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">마스크팩</span> (*maseukeupaek*).

## 10. <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">올인원</span> (*orinwon*) — all-in-one

A convenience label for multi-step-in-one products, popular in simplified
routines. Handy to know if you're short on time but still want the
multi-step benefits.

**Quick tip:** Once you recognize <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">에센스</span>,
<span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">앰플</span>, and
<span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">진정</span>, you'll be
able to navigate most Korean skincare shelves — whether you're shopping in
Seoul, ordering online, or reading ingredient lists on imported products.

## Put the words to work: a shopping scenario

Picture this: you've just walked into an Olive Young (Korea's biggest beauty
retail chain) and a store clerk approaches to ask if you need help. Here's
how these words might actually come up in conversation:

![Skincare shelves at an Olive Young store, with a shopper browsing the aisle](/images/posts/korean-skincare-olive-young-shelf.png)

<div class="not-prose space-y-4 my-6">
<div><strong>Clerk:</strong> <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">피부가 좀 예민하신가요?</span> <em>(pibuga jom yeminhasin-gayo?)</em><br>"Is your skin a bit sensitive?"</div>
<div><strong>You:</strong> <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">네, 저자극 제품을 찾고 있어요.</span> <em>(ne, jeojageuk jepumeul chatgo isseoyo.)</em><br>"Yes, I'm looking for a low-irritation/gentle product."</div>
<div><strong>Clerk:</strong> <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">이 제품은 진정 효과가 있어요.</span> <em>(i jepumeun jinjeong hyogwaga isseoyo.)</em><br>"This product has a soothing/calming effect."</div>
<div><strong>You:</strong> <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">좋아요, 수분도 많이 주나요?</span> <em>(joayo, subundo mani junayo?)</em><br>"Great, does it also give a lot of moisture/hydration?"</div>
<div><strong>Clerk:</strong> <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">네, 에센스 타입이라 흡수가 빨라요.</span> <em>(ne, esenseu taibira heupsuga ppallayo.)</em><br>"Yes, it's an essence type, so it absorbs quickly."</div>
<div><strong>You:</strong> <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">미백 효과도 있는 제품 있어요?</span> <em>(mibaek hyogwado inneun jepum isseoyo?)</em><br>"Do you have one with a brightening effect too?"</div>
<div><strong>Clerk:</strong> <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">이 앰플 한번 써보세요, 탄력에도 좋아요.</span> <em>(i aempul hanbeon sseobose-yo, tallyeogedo joayo.)</em><br>"Try this ampoule — it's also good for elasticity/firmness."</div>
<div><strong>You:</strong> <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">시트팩도 하나 살게요, 올인원 제품은 나중에 볼게요.</span> <em>(siteupaekdo hana salgeyo, orinwon jepumeun najunge bolgeyo.)</em><br>"I'll also buy a sheet mask — I'll look at the all-in-one products later."</div>
</div>

Bonus phrases to save for your next K-beauty run:

- <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">이거 저자극이에요?</span> *(igeo jeojageugieyo?)* — "Is this low-irritation/gentle?"
- <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">진정 제품 추천해 주세요.</span> *(jinjeong jepum chucheonhae juseyo.)* — "Please recommend a soothing product."
- <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">수분 크림 있어요?</span> *(subun keurim isseoyo?)* — "Do you have a moisturizing cream?"
- <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">미백에 좋은 에센스 있나요?</span> *(mibaege joeun esenseu innayo?)* — "Is there a brightening-effective essence?"
- <span class="font-korean font-semibold text-brand-600">탄력 앰플 써도 될까요?</span> *(tallyeok aempul sseodo doelkkayo?)* — "Can I try a firming ampoule?"

Even just recognizing these words on a shelf (without saying a word out
loud) will make shopping — in store or online — far less overwhelming.

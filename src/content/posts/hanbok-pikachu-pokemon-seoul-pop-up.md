If your feed has suddenly filled up with a pastel-orange Pikachu in a
tiny <a href="/vocab#hanbok" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">한복</a>
(*hanbok*), you're not imagining it. Pokémon Korea just announced its
next <a href="/vocab#papeop-seutoeo" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">팝업스토어</a>
(*papeop seutoeo*), pop-up store, and it's one of the more visually
striking ones yet.

## What it actually is

The event is called **"피카츄의 가을나들이"** — Pikachu's Autumn
<a href="/vocab#nadeuri" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">나들이</a>
(*nadeuri*), part of Pokémon Korea's larger "Mega Festa 2026." The
concept: Hanbok Pikachu wanders through a world styled after
<a href="/vocab#minhwa" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">민화</a>
(*minhwa*), traditional Korean folk painting — pine trees, a lotus
pond, a full moon — where, per the official announcement, the line
between reality and the painting itself starts to blur. Other Pokémon
show up in the scene too, each doing their own version of enjoying
autumn.

It's a strong pick for the season: minhwa motifs are exactly the kind
of imagery you'd see in a real folk painting, so the pop-up doubles as
a (very cute) intro to traditional Korean art style, not just a
merch drop.

## When and where

- **Dates:** September 18 – October 11, 2026
- **Location:** <a href="https://map.naver.com/p/entry/place/1884270356" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-brand-700 underline">Hiker Ground</a>, 40 Cheonggyecheon-ro, Jung-gu, Seoul (서울특별시 중구 청계천로 40) — tap for the Naver Map listing
- **Hours / activities:** not yet announced — Pokémon Korea's post says details are "to be revealed later," so check closer to opening if you're planning a visit

Hiker Ground sits right along the Cheonggyecheon stream in central
Seoul, an easy add-on if you're already spending a day around Jongno
or Euljiro.

## There's a Pokémon GO event running alongside it

The pop-up isn't standalone — Pokémon GO is running a matching
in-game event, and per the
<a href="https://pokemongo.com/news/pikachu-autumn-picnic-korea-2026" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-brand-700 underline">official event page</a>,
it's split into two overlapping pieces:

**Regional event ("가을 소풍"), Sep 18 – Oct 11** — limited to Seoul's
Jongno/Jung-gu area and Incheon Airport's Hiker Station:

- Orange Hanbok Pikachu appears in 1★ raids at 15 designated gyms in
  the zone (capped at once per gym per day, and remote raid passes
  don't work here)
- Free timed research around local PokéStops rewards 2,026 XP, a
  Hanbok Pikachu encounter, a Premium Battle Pass, and an event
  sticker — all with a boosted shiny (color-variant) chance
- Field research from event-area PokéStops can reward a Hanbok
  Pikachu encounter, an encounter with another event-themed Pokémon,
  and Stardust (one task per PokéStop per day)
- Bonuses: 2-hour Lure Modules and event-themed pollen effects on the
  map
- A separate **in-person Pokémon GO event** is listed as "to be
  announced" — worth watching for, since it'd mean an official Niantic
  activation on top of the Pokémon Korea pop-up itself

**Nationwide event ("피카츄의 한국 나들이"), Sep 24–26 only** — playable
anywhere in Korea:

- Same Hanbok Pikachu 1★ raids, no location or participation limits
- Free timed research rewards 4,500 XP, a Premium Battle Pass, 5,500
  Stardust, and a Hanbok Pikachu encounter
- Bonuses: 2× Candy for sending Pokémon to the Professor, 2× Stardust
  from raids, and a fireworks effect on the map

One quirk worth flagging if you're playing along: both timed research
tracks share the same claim deadline, **Oct 11, 20:00 KST** — even
though the nationwide event itself only runs Sep 24–26, you have until
the regional event's end date to actually turn in the quest and
collect the reward.

If you're a player as well as a visitor, it's worth timing your trip
to the pop-up around the regional event window so both experiences
line up.

## Good to know before you go

Pop-ups like this are a whole subculture in Seoul — brands treat them
as short-lived events rather than stores, and locals track them
closely enough that accounts like
<a href="https://www.instagram.com/trend._.high/" class="font-korean rounded bg-brand-50 px-1 font-semibold text-brand-700 no-underline hover:bg-brand-100">핫플</a>
(*hatpeul*) trackers exist specifically to flag the next one before it
opens. Expect lines, expect limited merch, and expect details (hours,
ticketing, whether it's reservation-only) to firm up closer to the
opening date rather than all at once.

We'll update this post once Pokémon Korea shares the specifics — for
now, pencil in September 18 and keep an eye on their official channels.
